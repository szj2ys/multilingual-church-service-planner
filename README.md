# Multilingual Church Service Planner

A small workbook for the person coordinating a service, language support, and the welcome team. Use it to decide what guests can follow, who prepares each part, and what happens when a plan fails. It does not provide translations, interpreting, or a prescribed order of worship.

Everything needed for planning is in these files. Download or copy them once; work in a spreadsheet, text editor, or on paper without an account or internet connection. Adapt the segments to your community's practices. This is an original planning aid, not a field-tested ministry program.

## Start with a ten-minute first pass

Have your service outline and team roles beside you. Ten minutes is a planning timebox, not enough time to prepare translations or complete a rehearsal. Missing information should remain visible.

1. **Minutes 0–2: define the need.** Ask expected participants which language and format help them take part. Do not infer this from appearance or nationality. Record confirmed needs and unanswered questions separately. Choose one coordinator who can change the service plan.
2. **Minutes 2–5: map the service.** Save a working copy of [the blank service plan](templates/service-plan.csv). It contains only column headings. Add one row per segment; duplicate a row if different languages need different support. Include arrivals, transitions, announcements, and departure as well as the sermon. Estimate minutes including interpretation pauses.
3. **Minutes 5–7: assign the work.** Give each row one responsible role. In your local team copy, assign a person to that role and confirm they agree. Identify the exact paper, file, equipment, or person needed. A proposed asset is not an available asset.
4. **Minutes 7–9: expose failure points.** Write each fallback as **Trigger / Decision owner / Action / Guest message**. Check a guest without a phone, unusable Wi-Fi, and an absent interpreter. Mark unfinished rows BLOCKED, with an owner, next action, and deadline.
5. **Minute 9–10: draft the handoff.** Copy proposed methods, limits, triggers, and named owners into a draft of [the welcome-team handoff](welcome-team-handoff.md). Schedule [the rehearsal checklist](rehearsal-checklist.md); finalize the handoff after rehearsal, before advertising language support. Review the [fictional example](templates/fictional-example.csv) only to understand the format; none of its people, assets, or arrangements exist.

## Fill the columns

| Column | What to record |
|---|---|
| service segment | A recognizable part of your service, in order. |
| duration estimate (minutes) | Total expected minutes, including pauses and transitions. |
| language | Spoken language and language needing support; specify the relevant variety when known. |
| access method | What a guest actually hears, reads, or receives; distinguish full interpretation from a summary. |
| responsible role | One accountable role, assigned to a person in the local copy. |
| asset | Exact item, version, location, reviewer, and available quantity where relevant. |
| fallback | Trigger / Decision owner / Action / Guest message. |
| readiness | UNREVIEWED, BLOCKED, or READY; add evidence or the next action, owner, and deadline. |

Import CSV as UTF-8 with comma separators. Keep each row at eight columns; a spreadsheet handles quoting when you save. For paper use, write the same eight headings across two pages if needed. Keep a dated master copy so the welcome team and service leader use the same version.

## Make the limits usable

A printed summary does not reproduce a live sermon, discussion, or last-minute announcement. Printed words also do not meet every reading or vision need. Ask whether the proposed format is usable; record the answer rather than assuming access.

If no competent interpreter or reviewed material is available, mark the affected language support BLOCKED. Do not label an unreviewed machine output as ready or ask a guest to become the interpreter on arrival. Explain the specific gap in a language the guest understands, using reviewed wording or a willing, competent adult helper if available. If you cannot establish understanding, record that communication gap and involve the coordinator; do not claim the guest has understood. Offer only choices the team can actually provide.

## Optional reading and reuse

These external readings are not needed to complete the workbook. They do not endorse it.

- Calvin Institute of Christian Worship, [Multilingual Worship: How to include many languages](https://worship.calvin.edu/resources/articles/multilingual-worship-how-include-many-languages), August 30, 2011: examples of communities planning together and using different languages across worship.
- Calvin Institute of Christian Worship, [Expand Your Church's Bilingual Music Repertoire](https://worship.calvin.edu/resources/articles/expand-your-churchs-bilingual-music-repertoire), October 9, 2015: approaches to bilingual songs and pronunciation preparation. Obtain appropriate permissions for any music you choose; this workbook includes no songs or Scripture texts.
